# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A real, repeating use.** A filter that wears out every 2 months gives the business a reason to talk to the customer again, and the subscription is a sensible way to handle it.
- **The supply chain is already mapped.** Bottle and filter suppliers are named, landed costs are known, a 3PL ships DDP to the door and the Shopify store is live. Few early ideas get this far on fulfilment.
- **The plan pricing nudges the right behaviour.** The bottle costs US$10 less with any plan (39.99 vs 49.99), renewals ship free, and the 3-pack plan ships the first filters with the bottle. On the 3-pack plan the customer gets a working system on day 0 and never thinks about filters again.
- **There is headroom on the bottle.** US$38.30 profit (61%) on a bottle-only order leaves room to spend on finish, packaging and unboxing without breaking the model.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The core claim is unproven.** The product is clean water, and "150 L per filter" rests only on the supplier's own test, with no lab report and no certified water-quality claims. The lens treats craft in the parts nobody sees as the product itself. If one customer, creator or regulator asks "filters what, certified by whom?" and there is no answer, the creator channel turns against the brand. The figure also needs matching to the 2-month life: 150 L / 60 days = 2.5 L a day. The founder should check whether that fits real use and the filter's tested limits.
2. **There is no one thing, only a cheaper LARQ.** The brief does not say whether the bottle is insulated, does not define the customer and does not give a one-sentence reason to choose Claro. The only stated difference is price: about 23% under LARQ's bottle and about 7% under its 3-pack plan. A product defined by a competitor's price list has no experience to work back from, and a lower price is the easiest advantage to lose.
3. **The scope is too wide and the seams are loose.** The plan covers six markets and five currencies at launch, with six SKU/price points, two subscription cadences, creator codes and 3PL handoffs. Delivery takes 4-8 working days and the EU time is TBC. Every handoff (creator to store, store to 3PL, 3PL to customs, renewal billing to shipping) is where the experience breaks. The single-filter plan is also the weakest unit: 47% margin in the US and 34-45% elsewhere, with a ~US$4 mailer on a US$15.29 order. It adds complexity for the smallest share of buyers (80 of 1,000 assumed).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is Claro in one sentence, without naming LARQ or a price?
- Is the bottle insulated? What does it feel like in the hand, and what does the first sip taste like next to tap water?
- Walk me through the first five minutes: the creator video, the checkout, the 4-8 day wait, the box opening, the first fill. Where does it feel worse than LARQ?
- What exactly does the filter remove, and when will an independent lab confirm the 150 L and any water-quality claims?
- What happens when a renewal arrives late, a card fails, or someone in the EU waits two weeks? Who owns that moment?
- Which one market and which one plan would you keep if you could launch only one?
- Open questions for other skills: market size and how crowded the space is beyond LARQ (Brita, Water-to-Go) -> /founder-competitors. Whether target buyers care about anything besides price, and the real buyer profile -> /founder-consumer. Real creator CAC and whether retention of 85% / 70% holds once ad cost is known -> /founder-cfo.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Proof before launch.** Get an independent lab report for filter capacity and every claim made about what it removes. Advertise only what is certified.
2. **Name the one thing.** Settle the bottle spec (insulated or not, size, finish), define the target customer, and write a one-sentence pitch that does not depend on undercutting LARQ.
3. **Cut to focus.** Launch in one market with one subscription (the 3-pack plan, which has the better margin and simpler logistics). Add markets and the single-filter plan only once the first one runs cleanly.
4. **Own the seams.** Map the full journey from creator post to second renewal, confirm 3PL delivery times for the launch market, and set a plan for late, failed and lost orders before the first creator goes live.
