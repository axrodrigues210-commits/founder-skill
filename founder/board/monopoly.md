# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **The numbers before ads are healthy.** The brief gives 47-61% margins per order and a 57% contribution. On the founder's own P&L, ad cost could rise from the assumed $15 per bottle to about $36 per bottle ($15 + $20,970 / 1,000) before year-1 profit reaches zero. That leaves room to learn what customers really cost.
- **One small gap is real.** LARQ sells no single-filter plan, so Claro's every-2-months single filter is something the reference competitor does not offer. It is small, but it is a place to start.
- **The cost of finding out is low.** The store is live, fixed costs are ~$120/month and the first batch costs ~$10,480 in stock. A cheap test can show whether a small market exists before much is spent.
- **Renewals could become a base.** The founder's forecast reaches ~500 active 3-pack subscribers by the end of year 2. Recurring customers are the only route the brief shows toward a brand.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **There is nothing proprietary, so anyone can copy it.** The bottle and filter come off the shelf from Coolway, and nothing in the brief says Claro has exclusive rights to them. Any brand, or Coolway itself, can sell the same product next to Claro. The only edge the brief claims is price: ~23% under LARQ's bottle and ~7% under its 3-pack plan. The Monopoly lens treats competing on price in a crowded category as the way profit gets destroyed. The one technical claim is 150 L per filter against LARQ's 94 L, about 1.6x better. That falls well short of the ~10x the lens looks for, and it is the supplier's own untested claim with no lab report. Nothing stops a copycat in month six.
2. **There is no small market to own first.** The target customer is "not yet defined", and the plan launches into six countries and five currencies at once. The lens wants a large share of a small, specific market. This plan starts with a tiny share of a huge one, against LARQ, Brita, Water-to-Go and other competitors nobody has mapped yet.
3. **Getting customers is unproven.** The ad cost ($15 per bottle), the share of orders using creator codes (25%) and retention (85% and 70%) are all ASSUMPTIONS. Creators are paid on the first order only, so they get nothing for bringing customers who stay. Nobody knows what creators actually ask for (fees, free product). The lens counts distribution as half the business, and here that half is a guess.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know about this market that LARQ and Brita do not? That is the secret. If the answer is "a cheaper price", that is not one.
- Which group of buyers, in one country, could Claro become the obvious choice for within 12 months? How many of them are there? (Market size is unknown. /founder-competitors and /founder-consumer can answer it.)
- How exactly do the first 100 customers arrive, and what does each one really cost? (Real ad cost per customer through creators is unknown. /founder-cfo can model it once a small test supplies the inputs.)
- Can you get an exclusive deal with Coolway, or your own filter design? Can you get a lab-certified capacity claim that a copycat cannot easily match?
- Why now? What has changed that makes this the moment for a new filtered-bottle brand?
- Who is the team, and how much time does it have? (The brief does not say.)
- In ten years, what would make a buyer choose Claro over an identical bottle that is $5 cheaper?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable (PASS). The vote would move toward FUND IF with three changes:
1. Pick one small market: one country and one specific buyer group. Show it can win a large share of that group.
2. Find a defensible difference worth roughly 10x on something buyers care about, for example an exclusive or certified filter. Being cheaper does not count.
3. Run a real test that shows how much a customer through creators actually costs and how many renew.
