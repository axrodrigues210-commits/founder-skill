# Board verdict: Claro

**Vote: 2 FUND IF, 1 PASS. Average score 3.7 / 10.**

| Lens | Score | Vote |
|---|---|---|
| Offers | 4 | FUND IF |
| Monopoly | 3 | PASS |
| Product | 4 | FUND IF |

Memos: `board/offers.md`, `board/monopoly.md`, `board/product.md`. Brief: `board/brief.md`.

## Risks raised by more than one member (most dangerous first)

1. **The only difference is price.** All three lenses raised this. Claro is described as a cheaper LARQ: about 23% under on the bottle, about 7% under on the 3-pack plan and level on the one-time 3-pack. A price edge is the easiest one for a competitor to take away. There is no one-sentence reason to choose Claro that doesn't mention LARQ or a price.
2. **The filter claim is unproven.** All three raised this too. "150 L" comes only from the supplier's own test. There's no lab report and no certified claim about what the filter removes. For a brand whose product is clean water, this weakens trust and conversion. It also exposes the brand to ad rejections, refunds and regulators in six markets, and to creators turning on it.
3. **No defined customer, and too much launch scope.** Raised by Monopoly and Product, with Offers asking who the buyer is. The plan opens in six markets with five currencies, six price points, two subscription cadences and an EU delivery time that is still TBC, with no specific buyer in mind.
4. **Getting customers is a guess.** Raised by Offers and Monopoly, with Product deferring it to the CFO. The $15 ad cost per bottle, the 25% creator-code share and the 85% / 70% retention rates are all assumptions. Creators are paid on the first order only, so they earn nothing for bringing customers who stay subscribed.

## Where the board disagrees

- **Fund or not.** Offers and Product would fund this as a cheap, testable experiment: about $10.5k of stock, about $120 a month fixed and a live store. Monopoly votes PASS because nothing can be defended. The bottle and filter are off the shelf from Coolway, and any brand, Coolway included, can sell the same thing.
- **The single-filter plan.** Offers counts it as a real gap, since LARQ has no single-filter plan. Product wants it cut at launch: it's the weakest unit (47% margin in the US, 34–45% elsewhere, about a $4 mailer on a $15.29 order) and it adds complexity for an assumed 8% of buyers.
- **Margin headroom.** Offers and Monopoly both note that year 1 still breaks even if ad cost reaches about **$36 per bottle**, 2.4 times the assumed $15. That's real room to learn, but only if blended acquisition cost actually comes in under it.

## Conditions checklist

- [ ] **Independent lab report** on filter capacity and everything the filter removes. Advertise only certified claims. (All three)
- [ ] **One target customer** defined (who, situation, the result they want), plus a **one-sentence pitch** that doesn't depend on undercutting LARQ. → `/founder-consumer` (All three)
- [ ] **Bottle spec settled**: insulated or not, size, finish. Re-run the LARQ comparison like for like. (Offers, Product)
- [ ] **Launch in one market** first, with one buyer group and the 3-pack plan as the default. Add markets and the single-filter plan once it runs cleanly. → `/founder-launch` (Monopoly, Product)
- [ ] **Map the competitors** beyond LARQ (Brita, Water-to-Go and others), plus market size. → `/founder-competitors` (All three)
- [ ] **An offer, not a discount**: a named guarantee the CFO approves, low-cost bonuses, and a test of whether it holds at or above LARQ's price. → `/founder-offer`, `/founder-pricing` (Offers)
- [ ] **Paid test on the first ~100 bottles** to measure the real cost per customer through creators and the real plan take-up. Continue only if blended acquisition cost stays well under ~$36 per bottle and more than 28% of buyers choose a plan. → `/founder-cfo` (Offers, Monopoly)
- [ ] **Find out what creators actually ask for** (flat fees, free product, commission rate) and decide whether to pay on renewals, not just first orders. (Offers, Monopoly)
- [ ] **A defensible edge** to look into: an exclusive deal with Coolway, your own filter design, or a certified performance claim a copycat can't easily match. (Monopoly)
- [ ] **Own the seams**: map the journey from creator post to second renewal, confirm 3PL delivery times for the launch market, and have a plan for late, failed and lost orders and failed renewal payments. → `/founder-ops` (Product)

## The strongest version the board can see

This is not the business as pitched. The pitch is six markets, two plans and a cheaper LARQ. The board's version is narrower: one market, one clearly named buyer, and a bottle sold as a **filter system on autopilot**. The bottle ships with the 3-pack plan by default, so the customer has four filters on day 0 and never thinks about them again. Every performance claim is backed by an independent lab report that LARQ's 94 L rating can be compared against, and a named guarantee covers the risk. Creators are paid for subscribers who stay, not just first orders, so they bring buyers who renew. Price stays competitive but is no longer the reason to buy. The first 100 bottles are a test that shows the real cost of a customer. Only once that number is under about $36 a bottle and plan take-up beats 28% does Claro add markets or the single-filter plan. Even then, the Monopoly lens's warning stands: unless Claro finds something a copycat can't easily buy from Coolway, this stays a good small business rather than a durable one.
